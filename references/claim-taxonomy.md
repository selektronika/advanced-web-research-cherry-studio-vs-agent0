# Claim Taxonomy

Classify every material claim along four axes. Keep the classification in the evidence
ledger; surface only what changes the answer or the label.

## Claim type

- **Factual** — a checkable state of affairs ("the company operates twelve vessels", "the
  act took effect on this date").
- **Causal** — X produces or contributes to Y. Requires design evidence, not co-occurrence.
- **Statistical** — a measured quantity, rate, or distribution. Requires a method, a
  population, a period, and an uncertainty statement.
- **Legal** — a question of statute, regulation, or judgment. Requires the text of the
  provision and its status (in force, amended, repealed, draft).
- **Historical** — a past event or process. Requires contemporaneous or archival evidence.
- **Scientific or technical** — an empirical or engineering claim. Requires underlying
  research, standards, or reproducible measurement.
- **Predictive** — a claim about the future. State whose forecast, what assumptions, and
  what track record.
- **Interpretive** — a reading of meaning or intent. Attribute the reading; do not present
  it as fact.
- **Experiential** — a first-person account. Valuable as testimony; never as prevalence.
- **Sentiment-based** — a reported attitude or mood. Sample and method matter.
- **Symbolic or traditional** — meaning carried by a tradition or symbol system. Describe
  the tradition accurately; label the layer.
- **Metaphysical** — a claim beyond empirical test. Neither prove nor disprove empirically.

## Evidence type

Official record · primary document · dataset · original research · systematic review ·
technical standard · expert analysis · reputable reporting · testimony · community report ·
social signal · unsupported assertion.

## Source relationship

Independent · derivative · same wire service or press release · interested party ·
promotional · anonymous · adversarial · methodologically weak.

## Epistemic status ladder

| Status | Meaning | Typical label in the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Established / directly documented | The primary record states it and was read | `[Documented]` |
| Strongly supported | Two or more genuinely independent origins agree | `[Supported]` |
| Moderately supported | Credible but limited, single-method, or partly indirect | `[Supported]` with the limit stated |
| Plausible but limited | Some evidence, weak method, or interested origin | `[Reported]` / `[Inferred]` with the limit |
| Disputed | Credible origins conflict | `[Disputed]` plus a contradiction table |
| Unresolved | Evidence insufficient and not yet searched out | `[Unverified]` |
| Unsupported | Asserted without retrievable evidence | `[Unverified]` and named as such |
| Not meaningfully testable | Outside empirical test | `[Traditional]` / `[Metaphysical]`, evaluated on its own standard |

## Matching standard to claim type

- Do not demand an empirical standard for a metaphysical, symbolic, or traditional claim;
  ask instead whether the tradition, text, or lineage is accurately represented.
- Do not accept testimony, prevalence language, or marketing for a factual, statistical,
  causal, legal, or financial claim.
- A dataset with a published method beats a well-written article. An audited filing beats a
  company summary. A registry beats a news estimate. A judgment beats commentary on it.
- Sentiment and lived-experience claims are answered with sentiment and lived-experience
  evidence — and labeled as such, never as fact about the world.
